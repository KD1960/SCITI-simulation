SCITI · Agent-based simulation · Ridge Line case

Who innovates with whom,
and does it pay?

SCITI 1 turns every firm in the Ridge Line supply chain into an agent that decides, each quarter, whether to adopt a supply chain technology — alone or with partners. It runs the chain week by week for three years and measures what changes: profit, cost, customer satisfaction, and CO2.

2Every week: run the chain

DisruptScheduled shocks cut capacity or add delay
ReceiveShipments arrive at the next tier
SellStores meet demand; unmet demand is lost
BuildFactories assemble when all 160 parts are on hand
OrderForecast, then order up to a target stock
ShipPick a mode; lead time, cost, CO2, defects
AccountProfit and cash per firm; safety checks

156 weeks. Technologies change the numbers inside these steps — e.g. better forecasts, lower shipping cost, fewer defects.

5What it measures

Economics

Profit by firm, tier, and network; cost by type; tech payback vs. a same-seed no-tech run

Customers

Fill rate, on-time delivery, delivery lead time, quality

Chain health

CO2 by lane and mode; bullwhip ratio per tier; who adopted what, when, and with whom

Customer satisfaction index = 0.5 × fill rate + 0.3 × on-time + 0.2 × quality

Built for research

  • One seed reproduces a run exactly
  • Paired baselines: same demand, no technology
  • Batch 30 seeds in ~90 s → one tidy results table
  • Exact replay of LLM runs from the decision log; spend caps

Built for teaching

  • World map with moving shipments by mode
  • Tech rings and group lines as firms adopt
  • Dashboard vs. the no-tech baseline
  • Click a firm to read why it decided
Honest label: technology costs and effects, prices, and markups are placeholder assumptions until backed by citations. Results today are illustrative, not estimates of real-world impact.
